## Language depends on what the archive makes readable

In the Calhoun native-text baseline, the smoothed log₂ early-to-late token-rate ratios for *security* and *agile* were −3.83 and −8.70, respectively. The catalog-issued cohorts use years through {{LANG_EARLY_MAX}} and from {{LANG_LATE_MIN}} onward. The direction held in the wrapper-excluded and native-rich views, while the eligible issue counts changed from {{LANG_NATIVE_EARLY_ISSUES}} and {{LANG_NATIVE_LATE_ISSUES}} in raw native text to 24 and 134 in the native-rich view. The targeted OCR view also placed *agile* more often in the later cohort, with a log₂ ratio of −10.00 across {{LANG_OCR_EARLY_ISSUES}} early and {{LANG_OCR_LATE_ISSUES}} late issues. The estimates describe different observed text populations. The figure's {{LANG_RESAMPLES}} within-view issue resamples give marginal diagnostics conditional on those populations.

On the 279-PDF topic checkpoint, access to scanned prose changed both the amount of evidence and the apparent language distribution. Among issues dated 1989–1999, adding historical OCR raised the issue-weighted delivery and process phrase rate from 8.6% to 24.0% while the pooled page rate fell from 34.1% to 27.0%. Within the 82 issues available in both modes, the mean paired increase was 20.0 percentage points. Trimming the outer three physical pages at each end changed the early issue-weighted rate by just 0.1 percentage points among 104 matched issues. The two weighting schemes expose how page opportunities and issue representation affect observed prominence.

The same checkpoint placed cybersecurity phrases on 22.8% of eligible pages in the average 2010–2019 issue. Intelligent-systems phrases reached 17.3% in the average 2020–2025 issue across 11 PDFs and five observed years. Year-block reweighting put the latter rate between 4.9% and 30.2% for the central 95% of resamples. Removing one year at a time gave 6.2% to 19.9%. Dictionary sensitivity formed a separate check. Removing *artificial intelligence* yielded intelligent-systems rates of 3.5% in 1989–1999 and 14.1% in 2020–2025. Removing *quality assurance* reduced the overall assurance rate from 8.3% to 3.4%. The recent-frame interval is wide, and the assurance contrast shows how strongly some families depend on individual phrases.

Independent implementations agreed on the phrase-screen output for 207 selected pages. Of 18 examined positive contexts, eight were substantive, three bibliographic, three publication furniture, and four unclear. These bounded checks support reproducibility of the counted pattern and expose ambiguity in its interpretation. The native-text contrasts similarly surfaced plausible domain words alongside extraction fragments. The results characterize words in the observed text and leave the prevalence of actual engineering practices to article-level evidence.

## Page structure and publication form

The position of text within an issue mattered for some phrase families. Across 277 PDFs the mean within-issue difference was +7.6 percentage points for delivery and process and +6.3 points for measurement. Central 95% whole-PDF resampling ranges were +5.4 to +9.7 and +5.0 to +7.7 points, respectively. The corresponding cybersecurity difference was +0.3 points with a range of −1.2 to +1.7. Page position therefore distinguishes some vocabulary families in this source frame, and its value varies by family.

In 244 PDFs with a qualified front and 7,617 eligible later pages, the median front was 68.9 percentage points darker than its issue’s median later page. After whole-page darkness was removed, the mean front-minus-interior center-to-ring contrast was −4.4 points (central 95% whole-PDF resampling range −5.8 to −3.0). The direction held for magazine covers and newsletter fronts when examined separately. A distinct issue-weighted grouping of all {{SHAPE_PAGES}} pages from {{SHAPE_ISSUES}} retained issues achieved held-out adjusted Rand agreement of {{SHAPE_ARI_MIN}} to {{SHAPE_ARI_MAX}} across five refits. The groups capture reproducible spatial patterns in the rendered pages. Those patterns contain contributions from layout, illustration, and scanning, so semantic interpretation still requires page inspection.
